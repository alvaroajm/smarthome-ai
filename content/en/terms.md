## 2. Electrical work, hardware and firmware

Some guides describe procedures that carry **real risk** when done incorrectly:

- **Mains electricity.** Switches, sockets, relays and in-wall modules connect to 110–240 V mains.
  Electrical installation, replacement and repair must be carried out by a **qualified
  electrician**, with power disconnected at the breaker and in accordance with your local
  electrical code and regulations. Electric shock, short circuits and fire are possible
  consequences of a wrong connection or an uncertified product.
- **Hardware and firmware.** Flashing firmware (ESPHome, Tasmota, WLED, Zigbee2MQTT, OpenThread),
  replacing a Zigbee coordinator, opening devices or changing their settings may **void the
  warranty**, brick the device or cause unexpected behaviour. Do it at your own risk, with a
  backup first, following the project's official documentation.
- **Automations.** Automations that operate locks, gates, heaters, pumps, irrigation, appliances
  or any load capable of causing damage must have independent safeguards (thermostats, timers,
  physical limits) and must never be the only safety mechanism protecting people, animals or
  property.
- **Internet exposure.** Exposing Home Assistant, cameras, servers or dashboards to the internet
  without strong authentication, HTTPS and regular updates may allow unauthorised access to your
  home. The guides describe recommended practices, but the security of your network is your
  responsibility.

Before following any procedure, read it in full, check that it applies to your equipment, software
versions and region, and stop if anything does not match the description.
